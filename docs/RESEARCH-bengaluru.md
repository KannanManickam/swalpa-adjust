# Bengaluru traffic: research brief for a civic-sense web experience (as of 14 Oct 2026)

**How to read this:**
- **[V]** means I checked the figure in the primary data or the full article.
- **[S]** means it comes from a secondary report or a search summary. Re-check these before publishing.
- **⚠** marks a figure where sources conflict or the year is uncertain.
- Most Deccan Herald (DH) pages blocked direct fetching (HTTP 403), so DH figures marked [S] come from search snippets or from outlets that carried the same story.

## 1. Headline stats (TomTom Traffic Index)

**2025 edition (released 20–22 Jan 2026)**
- **Second most congested city in the world**, behind Mexico City (75.9), with Dublin third. Bengaluru's congestion level is 74.4, up from a restated 72.7 for 2024. [V] TomTom Newsroom, 20 Jan 2026, https://www.tomtom.com/newsroom/explainers-and-insights/tomtom-traffic-index-2026-headline-numbers/
- **3 min 37 s per km**, against 2 min 4 s in free-flow conditions. That works out to roughly 36 min per 10 km. [V] same source.
- **36 min 9 s per 10 km**, which is 2 min 4 s more than 2024. **About 168 h 40 min lost in rush hour per year**, roughly 13 hours more than 2024. **Rush-hour speed 13.9 km/h**, about 1 km/h slower. Bengaluru ranked 6th in 2023 and 3rd in 2024. [V] Deccan Chronicle, 22 Jan 2026, https://deccanchronicle.com/nation/bengaluru-named-worlds-second-most-congested-city-in-2025-1931959
  - ⚠ The Hindu (summarised by VisionIAS) and Down To Earth give the speed as 16.6 km/h rather than 13.9. Use TomTom's own page for the final number: https://www.tomtom.com/traffic-index/bengaluru-traffic (it did not render when I fetched it).
- **Also third-slowest city** on travel time. Kolkata is fourth. India is the 5th most congested country. [S] Down To Earth, 27 Jan 2026, https://www.downtoearth.org.in/amp/story/urbanisation/bengaluru-kolkata-among-worlds-slowest-cities-as-india-ranks-high-on-congestion-index
- **Other Indian cities in 2025:**
  - Pune: 5th globally, 152 h lost.
  - Mumbai: 18th, 126 h lost, 20.8 km/h.
  - New Delhi: 23rd, 60.2%, 104 h lost.
  - Kolkata: 29th, 58.9%, 35 min 18 s per 10 km, 14.3 km/h.
  - Hyderabad: 47th.
  - [V] VisionIAS/The Hindu, 21 Jan 2026: https://visionias.in/current-affairs/upsc-daily-news-summary/article/2026-01-21/the-hindu/society/indias-traffic-chokehold-bengaluru-ranks-second-pune-fifth-in-global-congestion-index-for-2025 and https://www.tomtom.com/traffic-index/kolkata-traffic/

**2024 edition (released Jan 2025)**
- **Third slowest in the world at 34 min 10 s per 10 km.** Barranquilla (36:06) and Kolkata (34:33, India's slowest) were ahead. Pune was 4th at 33:22. Bengaluru lost **117 h** in rush hour. [S] Patrika, https://patrika.com/en/national-news/slowest-city-kolkata-bengaluru-ranked-indias-slowest-cities-check-jaipur-delhi-mumbai-rankings-19311224?amp=1, and DH, https://www.deccanherald.com/india/karnataka/bengaluru/bengaluru-third-slowest-city-in-the-world-3352211
  - ⚠ The News Minute printed 30 min 10 s (13 Jan 2025). This looks like a typo.
- **2023 edition:** 6th, about 28 min 10 s per 10 km, 132 h lost. [S] Business Today, Feb 2024.
- ⚠ **Do not compare the editions directly.** TomTom changed its method in the 2025 edition. The 2024 edition reported congestion of about 38%, but the 2025 edition restates 2024 as 72.7. Likewise, 117 h and 168 h are not like-for-like. The 2025 edition implies a restated 2024 figure of about 155 h.

## 2. Vehicle population

- **About 1.3 crore registered vehicles as of 30 Aug 2026.** That includes more than 88.5 lakh two-wheelers (about 68%) and nearly 25.5 lakh cars. August 2026 added 76,508 registrations (about 2,470 a day), and 17.5% of them were EVs. [V] Auto Punditz citing Transport Dept/TOI, 29 Sep 2026, https://www.autopunditz.com/post/bengaluru-ev-registrations-august-2026-karnataka
- **As of 30 Jun 2026:** 1.28 crore vehicles, 25.2 lakh private cars, about 87 lakh two-wheelers, and about 3 lakh yellow-board taxis.
  - June 2026 added 78,084 vehicles. The record month was **Oct 2025 at 86,014**, after the GST cut.
  - Bengaluru has the most registered cars and two-wheelers in India.
  - [V] Business Today, 20 Jul 2026, https://www.businesstoday.in/auto/story/bengaluru-now-has-over-25-lakh-cars-heres-why-more-residents-are-ditching-public-transport-544058-2026-07-20
- **Mix as of 31 Mar 2026:** about 86 lakh two-wheelers, 24.8 lakh cars, **3.8 lakh autorickshaws**, and 2.9 lakh cabs.
  - FY2025-26 added **7.9 lakh vehicles, about 2,100 a day**: 5.2 lakh two-wheelers and 1.5 lakh cars.
  - The fleet was 64.4 lakh in 2015, so it has roughly doubled.
  - [V] News Karnataka, 10 Apr 2026, https://newskarnataka.com/karnataka/bengaluru-vehicle-count-crosses-1-2-crore-congestion-worsens/10042026/ and 7 Apr 2026, https://newskarnataka.com/bengaluru/bengalurus-vehicle-surge-doubles-pressure-on-limited-road-space/07042026/
- **FY2024-25:** 1.23 crore vehicles, with 7.22 lakh added. Density was 823 vehicles per km, up from 761. [S] DH, 2025, https://www.deccanherald.com/india/karnataka/bengaluru/bengaluru-adds-722l-new-vehicles-total-hits-123-cr-3484861
- **Roads:**
  - The Greater Bengaluru Authority network is **14,063 km**, which gives **about 900 vehicles per km**. Delhi has about 480. [V] Outlook Business, 20 Jul 2026, https://www.outlookbusiness.com/economy-and-policy/128-mn-vehicles-1-metro-network-inside-bengalurus-mobility-challenge
  - Asian Development Bank (Dec 2024): about 1 km of road per 1,000 people in Bengaluru, against a national average of 5. [S] Down To Earth.
- ⚠ **Population is about 14 million (estimate).** Registrations include vehicles no longer on the road, so ratios such as "almost one vehicle per resident" measure pressure, not actual traffic.

## 3. Economic cost

- **₹19,725 crore a year** (about ₹20,000 crore). The study was led by traffic expert **M N Sreehari** and presented to Deputy CM D K Shivakumar.
  - It counts delays, signal stoppages, slow vehicles mixing with fast ones, fuel, and occupants' time valued by salary.
  - It notes 60+ flyovers that are narrow and lack entry and exit lanes.
  - [V] The News Minute, 7 Aug 2023, https://www.thenewsminute.com/karnataka/bengalurus-traffic-jams-cost-rs-20000-crore-annually-study-180727
- **About ₹38,000 crore a year in social cost** for Bengaluru. This is part of ₹1.44 lakh crore across Delhi, Mumbai, Bengaluru and Kolkata.
  - The study is BCG's "Unlocking Cities", commissioned by Uber.
  - Bengaluru's peak-hour congestion was 162%.
  - [S] Business Today, 19 Apr 2018, https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/latest/economy-politics/story/india-wastes-as-much-as-rs-1-44-lakh-crore-due-to-traffic-congestion-says-uber-study-104587-2018-04-19, and DH editorial 2018.
  - ⚠ DH also cites ₹37,000 crore elsewhere.
- **Late arrivals alone cost 7.07 lakh productive hours, about ₹1,170 crore, in 2018.** [V] Vijayalakshmi S & Krishna Raj, ISEC Working Paper 554, 2022, https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:sch:wpaper:554
- ⚠ **BDA Revised Master Plan 2031:** about 60 crore person-hours lost a year (₹3,700 crore) and about 2.8 lakh litres of fuel wasted per hour. These figures are only quoted secondhand, with unit inconsistencies. Verify against the plan before using them.

## 4. Hotspots

The compact table with coordinates is at the end.

- **Hebbal** (ORR × NH-44, airport road):
  - Seven lanes used to converge into two at the flyover.
  - After the KR Puram up-ramp was dismantled in April 2025, ASTraM (Bengaluru Traffic Police's traffic-data system) recorded queues growing from 1–3 km to **12 km by mid-June 2025**. [S] DH, https://www.deccanherald.com/amp/story/india/karnataka/bengaluru/hebbal-junction-daily-commute-worsens-as-flyover-metro-work-drag-on-3126079
  - The Nagawara–Mekhri loop opened Aug 2025 and cut congestion by **31%**. A second loop from the airport side to Mekhri opened as a trial on 20 Dec 2025 (−25%) and was inaugurated Jan 2026.
  - The new choke point is the **Baptist Hospital bus stop at the descent, where five streams merge and buses block two lanes**. The tailback now reaches Mekhri Circle. [S] DH, Aug 2025–Jan 2026.
  - The Esteem Mall–Mekhri loop (₹300 crore) was promised for Nov 2025. ⚠ I could not confirm it has opened.
  - The Federal (27 Apr 2026) calls Hebbal the city's most congested junction.
- **Central Silk Board** (ORR × Hosur Rd):
  - South India's first road-cum-metro double-decker. **All five flyovers (A–E, 3.2 km) were operational on 25 Apr 2026.** The Yellow Line runs on the upper deck. Builder Afcons claims at least 30 min saved. [V] The Federal, 27 Apr 2026, https://thefederal.com/category/states/south/karnataka/bengaluru-silk-board-double-decker-flyover-fully-functional-240816
  - After ramps A–C opened in July 2024, congestion alerts fell from 24 to 15 a day and average queues from 19 km to 10 km. [V] Indiablooms, 6 Oct 2024, citing DCP Kuldeep Kumar Jain.
  - It is still the Blue Line terminal. Pillar-work barricades have been up since 10 Jan 2026, and the viaduct needs about 414 m more.
  - Waterlogging has reached up to 2 ft (Aug 2024).
- **Veerannapalya** (ORR, near Nagawara/BEL): **15.8 km average evening queue (5–9 pm)**, the worst in the city. The cause is Blue Line metro construction. [S] DH, 20 Dec 2025, https://www.deccanherald.com/india/karnataka/bengaluru/bengalurus-top-most-congested-traffic-hotspots-veerannapalya-gokaldas-junctions-3838215
- **Gokaldas Images Jn** ("North Bengaluru"): **9.9 km morning queue (8 am–12 pm)**, same DH article. ⚠ I could not verify its exact location, so it has no coordinates.
- **Goraguntepalya** (ORR × Tumakuru Rd/NH-48):
  - Inflow from about 20 districts, spillback from the CMTI signal, BMTC buses stopping at the roadside, and metro work.
  - Extra lanes and a free left are being added in 2026, but the signal wait is expected to persist. [S] DH 2026, Citizen Matters (older).
  - A 4.5 km tunnel road is proposed.
- **ORR tech corridor (Silk Board–Iblur–Bellandur/Ecospace–Marathahalli–KR Puram):**
  - The return to five-day office weeks in Oct 2025 brought a surge in single-occupancy cars. [S] DH, Oct 2025.
  - In Sep 2025, one BMTC breakdown near Ecospace, combined with Panathur white-topping work, caused a standstill of more than 4 hours.
  - About 70 vehicles break down on the ORR every day. [V] TNM, 20 Feb 2025.
  - The CM asked Wipro to open its Sarjapur campus road, estimating about 30% relief near Iblur. Azim Premji declined on 24 Sep 2025. [S] Business Today, 23 Sep 2025.
  - The Blue Line Silk Board–KR Puram trial runs were targeted for about Oct 2026. Work is still pending at Marathahalli and Silk Board, and the Iblur station has gas-pipeline conflicts.
- **KR Puram / Tin Factory:** The pillar of the cable-stayed bridge narrows the junction, buses halting at the ramp back traffic up, and national-highway heavy vehicles add load. [S] DH, older reports, ⚠ dated.
- **Whitefield / Hope Farm:** Ranked the worst of 142 recurring East Bengaluru bottlenecks, at **+4.3 min per km**. Causes are metro pillars cutting turning room, buses halting in the running lane, and broken medians used for U-turns. [V] TraffiCure (commercial; Google road-speed data, 5 May–5 Jun 2026), https://trafficure.com/case-studies/bangalore
- **Kundalahalli Gate:** Incomplete underpass work drives morning congestion. [S] Joint Commissioner Karthik Reddy, DH, Dec 2025.
- **Horamavu:** The signal approach is squeezed to one lane by barricades, with about 1 km queues (Jul 2026). This junction also had the most violations booked in one earlier 3-month period (8,293). [S] DH.
- **Bannerghatta Rd (Dairy Circle–Jayadeva):**
  - Closed one way for about 4 years for Pink Line underground stations. It has reopened, but police expect new bottlenecks at Dairy Circle. [S] DH, ~late 2025.
  - The elevated Pink Line stretch is awaiting CMRS authorisation (see section 7).
- **Madiwala / Hosur Rd (NH-44 inflow from Tamil Nadu and Kerala):** Trial diversions run 8–10:30 am. [S] DH, ⚠ year inferred as Aug 2026.
- **Waterlogging:** A May 2025 review counted 209 flood-prone spots, with 43 still pending. Named spots include Silk Board, Ecospace/Bellandur, the Panathur rail underpass, Hebbal, Nagawara–Hebbal on the ORR, and Veerasandra/Electronic City. [S]
- **Old Airport Rd, Mysore Rd, Yeshwanthpur:** I found no 2025–26 sourced cause. The Old Airport Road congestion reports are from 2023 (ORR traffic diverted there by metro work). ⚠

What the table shows:
- **No helmet accounts for 45% of all 2025 cases.**
- **Lane-discipline bookings collapsed from 3.88 lakh in 2018 to 1,522 in 2024.** Enforcement has shifted to cameras, which do not book this offence.
- The table has **no category for wrong-side driving, stop-line or zebra encroachment, yellow-box violations, or blocking a free left.** They are likely counted under "Others" or "signal jumping".

**Fines and contactless enforcement:**
- 2025 fines totalled **₹258.2 crore**, against ₹84.9 crore in 2024. Camera-booked ("automation") cases were 61.4 lakh of 69.9 lakh, or **88%**. [V] same dataset.
- DH reports ₹251 crore as a decade high, helped by two 50% rebate windows. ⚠ Small mismatch with the dataset.
- About 87% of violations in Jan–Jul 2025 were caught by AI cameras. [S] Amar Ujala, 14 Oct 2025.
- The cabinet approved **adaptive signals at 110 junctions** in Sep 2025. [S] The Week, 25 Sep 2025.

**Wrong side, rash driving and footpaths:**
- **Criminal (BNS) cases for rash or negligent driving and obstructing traffic, including wrong-direction driving: 6,872 in 2025**, up 82% from 3,774. Karthik Reddy said the police use BNS s.285 when wrong-side driving causes pile-ups; otherwise they issue a challan. [V] News Karnataka/DH, 15 Jan 2026, https://newskarnataka.com/bengaluru/rash-and-negligent-driving-cases-rise-82-in-bengaluru-in-2025/15012026/
- **About 83,000 booked for parking on footpaths and 14,700 for riding on them in one year (97,700 in all).** BTP has asked the GBA for 101 skywalks, 363 zebra crossings and 45 pelican signals. [S] DH, Dec 2025.
- Cases against **delivery riders** rose from 30,968 (2023) to 52,153 (2024) and 63,718 (2025, to 15 Nov). [S] DH.
- **Proposed ₹5,000 fine for footpath riding**, up from ₹500, raised by Minister Krishna Byre Gowda. It is a proposal, not yet law. [V] Asianet, 23 Jul 2026.
- 95 road-rage cases in 2025, 31 of them in Whitefield. [S] DH.

**Campaigns and official comments:**
- **Airport Road lane-discipline rule (16 Jul 2024):** heavy vehicles keep left, two-wheelers left or middle, overtaking on the right only; ₹500 fine. [S] DH.
- **Suraksha-75 junction redesign** (Greater Bengaluru Authority, BTP and WRI India; ₹150 crore): only one junction was fully completed per GBA data. [S] DH, ~2025.
- **Joint Commissioner (Traffic) M N Anucheth** said: "A single bus breakdown on a flyover can paralyse traffic for kilometres." He also said 12 corridors totalling 220 km carry about 60% of traffic. [V] TNM, 20 Feb 2025, https://www.thenewsminute.com/amp/story/karnataka/bengaluru-roads-are-choking-but-expanding-them-is-not-the-answer-says-top-traffic-cop
  - Other officials' remarks are paraphrased in this brief, to stay within quotation limits.
- ⚠ **"Keep Free Left Free":** I could not find this as a named BTP campaign, or any free-left case counts. A DCP has said a left turn is free only where signed, and drivers get booked for signal jumping at "no free left" junctions such as Siddalingaiah Circle (DH, older). Treat the phrase as your own messaging, not a BTP slogan.

## 6. Pedestrian safety

- **2025:** 896 fatal crashes (−3%) and 4,228 non-fatal (+4%). [V] DH via News Karnataka, 15 Jan 2026.
  - Jan–Oct 2025: 715 deaths and **203 pedestrian deaths**. [S] DH, Dec 2025.
  - ⚠ Full-year 2025 pedestrian deaths of **257** come from Siasat, 7 Jul 2026, which does not name its source.
- **2024:** 4,784 crashes and 893 deaths. Pedestrian deaths were 233 by BTP's count, or **246 (27% of 894 deaths)** in MoRTH's *Road Accidents in India 2024*. Under MoRTH, Bengaluru was **second among million-plus cities**, after Delhi. [S] DH.
- **2023:** **921 deaths, the highest in a decade.** Pedestrians, motorcyclists and cyclists made up 91% (BTP–Vital Strategies Road Safety Report 2023). NCRB counted **292 pedestrian deaths, the highest of 53 cities**, for the second year running. [S] DH; [V] TNM, 1 Oct 2025.
  - ⚠ A DH breakdown of NCRB data for 2023 gives two-wheelers 55% and pedestrians 32%.
- **Jan–Jun 2026:** 136 pedestrians killed and 664 injured. [V] Siasat.
- **Takeaway:** roughly **1 in 4 road deaths is a pedestrian**, and about 2.5 people die on the city's roads every day.

## 7. Cultural hooks

- **"Swalpa adjust maadi" = ಸ್ವಲ್ಪ ಅಡ್ಜಸ್ಟ್ ಮಾಡಿ**: ಸ್ವಲ್ಪ (swalpa, "a little") + ಅಡ್ಜಸ್ಟ್ (English "adjust") + ಮಾಡಿ (maadi, polite "do"). It means "please adjust a little" and is known as a Bengaluru catchphrase, for example on a crowded bus. [S] Holidify, https://www.holidify.com/blog/slangs-of-bangalore/
  - Kannada film *Swalpa Adjust Madkolli* (2000, dir. Kodlu Ramakrishna). [V] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swalpa_Adjust_Madkolli
  - ⚠ The film title in Kannada script was not verified.
- **Namma Metro:** 96.1 km and 83 stations on three lines. Ridership averaged 9.16 lakh a day (Apr 2026), with a record of 10.48 lakh on 11 Aug 2025. [S] Wikipedia.
  - **Purple** (East–West, Whitefield–Challaghatta) and **Green** (North–South): together about 77 km, operational.
  - **Yellow** (RV Road–Bommasandra via Silk Board and Electronic City, about 19 km): opened 10 Aug 2025 with 3 trains and 25-min gaps. By 2026 it had 13 of 15 trains, running every 7 min at peak. [S] DH 2026.
  - **Pink** (Kalena Agrahara–Nagawara, 21 km): the 7.5 km elevated stretch along Bannerghatta Rd **had its CMRS (metro safety commissioner) inspection 6–8 Oct 2026 and is awaiting authorisation**. It may open around Dasara and would take the network to 103.6 km. The underground section is targeted for about 2027. [V] Metro Rail News, 13 Oct 2026, https://metrorailnews.in/bengaluru-metro-pink-line-pm-inauguration-date/
  - **Blue** (Silk Board–KR Puram–Hebbal–airport, 58 km): under construction; phased opening from mid-2027, full line **March 2028**. [S] ConstructionWorld, 2026.
- **BMTC:** about 7,000 buses (Apr 2026). ⚠ Ridership is about 40–42 lakh a day, but the source dates are unclear.
- **Autos:** about 3.8 lakh registered (Mar 2026).
